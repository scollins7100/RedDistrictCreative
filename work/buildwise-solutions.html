The short version

As recently as July 2025, buildwiseohio.com was a parked placeholder page. In the two months of August and September 2026, Google showed BuildWise in search results a total of 4 times. After I set up its search foundation and submitted its sitemap on September 24, Google showed it 60 times and sent its first 12 clicks in just the first 10 days of October.

That's a small number with a big meaning: for the first time, a business owner searching for construction help could find BuildWise.

Why B2B search works differently

A dentist gets found by thousands of people searching "dentist near me." A construction project manager gets found by a few dozen business owners a year, each about to spend hundreds of thousands of dollars on a build-out. Every one of those searches matters, and each person does a lot of research before calling.

For a firm like this, the website isn't a storefront. It's the portfolio a prospect reviews before they ever pick up the phone. And Google, LinkedIn and AI tools can only recommend that portfolio if they can find it, read it and trust it. When I started, none of them could.

What I did

1. Built the search foundation

I verified the site in Google Search Console and submitted its sitemap on September 24, 2026. The sitemap is the list of pages a site hands to Google. Search Console is how a business tells Google "this site is mine, here's what's on it," and how it sees what Google thinks of it.

Why it connects: Google can't rank a page it hasn't found. Within days of the submission, BuildWise went from 4 appearances in two months to 60 appearances and 12 clicks in ten days.

2. Turned finished projects into proof

I standardized project pages into one consistent design and published three: Haverkos Family Orthodontics, Harrison Pediatric and White Oak Kids Dental, with a fourth (Delhi) next in line. I also worked with the team on a client intake form to capture details from each completed project.

Why it connects: project pages are what a prospect reads before calling, and what Google and AI tools use to understand what kind of work the firm does. A page titled with a real dental practice's name and project details answers the question "has this firm built something like mine?"

3. Started the Google Business Profile

I created BuildWise's Google Business Profile. Google requires a live video verification on site, with business signage, before it goes public.

Why it connects: a verified profile puts BuildWise on Google Maps and in local results, and it's one of the sources AI assistants check when someone asks for a local construction project manager.

4. Wrote the scripts for four project videos

I wrote scripts and recording prompts for four project videos, ready for production once the owner's voiceover is recorded.

Why it connects: video is the most convincing proof a construction firm can show, and it travels well on LinkedIn, BuildWise's primary channel.

5. Put the brand on the road and on paper

I managed two truck wrap designs with the wrap vendor, an F-150 (installed October 8) and an F-350, and documented BuildWise's logo, messaging, assets and positioning.

Why it connects: branded trucks parked at job sites are local advertising that runs every day. Searches for a company's name are a strong signal to Google, and a truck in a parking lot is often where that search starts.

6. Connected it to reporting

I added BuildWise to the Metricool reporting and scheduling setup in August, alongside its LinkedIn page and Search Console data, so its progress could be tracked month to month.

BuildWise is at the very start of its search life. These are the gaps that will decide how fast it grows.

What would bring in more clients

  • Publish what the team knows. Short articles answering the questions dentists ask before a build-out (timelines, budgets, permits, choosing a contractor) are exactly what Google and AI tools surface for early research.
  • Make every finished project a marketing event. Photos, a client quote, a project page, a LinkedIn post and a video. One project, five pieces of proof.
  • Ask every client for a Google review as soon as the profile is verified.
  • Lean into the niche. Dental and healthcare build-outs are a specific, valuable specialty. Owning that phrase in search is far more achievable than ranking for "construction company."
