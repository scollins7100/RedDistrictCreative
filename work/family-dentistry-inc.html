The short version

In four months, I moved the practice to a new domain without losing its search rankings, fixed a branding error that had the web agency's name showing in Google, rewrote the pages patients find in search, rebuilt the Franklin office page around new patients, expanded the structured data that Google and AI tools read, and built the first reporting system that showed the owners what their marketing was doing. Bookings and calls from Google search rose 28%, with no advertising budget.

Four months is early for search work. These results are the foundation, not the ceiling.

How a patient finds a dentist now

To understand this work, you have to understand the path a new patient takes. It almost never starts on the practice's website. It starts on Google, and increasingly in an AI answer.

Every step depends on information the practice provides. Google doesn't know a dentist is open Saturdays, accepts new patients, or handles emergencies unless the website and Google Business Profile say so clearly and consistently. If the information is missing or contradicts itself, Google shows someone else.

AI tools work the same way, only stricter. When someone asks an AI assistant for "a family dentist in Lebanon, Ohio that takes new patients," it builds the answer from sources it can read with confidence: structured data, clear page content and consistent business details. A practice that isn't machine-readable is invisible to those answers. That's why so much of my work at FDI was invisible to the eye and visible to Google.

What I did

1. Moved the domain without losing rankings

The practice moved from familydentistryincoh.com to fdiohio.dental. I made sure every old address redirects to its exact matching new page: the Franklin page to the new Franklin page, the services page to the new services page, and so on, rather than dumping everything on the homepage.

Why it connects: Google's rankings are attached to specific addresses. Page-to-page redirects tell Google "this page moved here," so the rankings move with it. Many domain moves lose a large share of their traffic at this step. This one held, and search impressions grew 12% through the move.

2. Fixed what patients see in Google

I removed the agency's name from the homepage title and rewrote it as "Family Dentistry, Inc. | Family Dentist in Lebanon & Franklin, OH." I rewrote the emergency dentistry page's description for same-day emergency searches.

Why it connects: the title and description are the practice's ad in Google's results, and they cost nothing. They decide whether someone clicks your listing or the one below it.

3. Rebuilt the Franklin office page around new patients

On September 28, I rebuilt the Franklin page as "Dentist in Franklin, OH 45005," with the street, phone number, the services new patients look for, and a clear path to book online in both the page and its Google description.

Why it connects: Google matches local searches to pages that clearly name the place and the service. "Dentist in Franklin, OH 45005" matches exactly what people type.

4. The AI piece: made the practice machine-readable

This is the work no one could see, and it may matter most over the next few years.

  • Expanded the structured data across the site. Schema is code that tells Google and AI tools facts directly: this is a dentist, here are its two addresses, its hours, its services, its ratings, and the cities and state it serves. I added service-area data (cities, state) and images to what was there.
  • Refreshed articles for AI answers. I updated the practice's articles so they answer patient questions plainly and completely, the format AI Overviews and assistants pull from.
  • Measured AI traffic for the first time. Visits from AI assistants went from zero before June to 1, 2, 4 and then 6 a month from June through September. Small numbers, but it's a brand new channel, and the practice is now in it.

Why it connects: AI tools don't browse a website the way a person does. They read structured facts and clear answers. A practice that provides them gets recommended. One that doesn't, doesn't get mentioned at all.

5. Built a reporting system from nothing

I connected Google Analytics 4, Search Console (through Supermetrics), both Google Business Profiles and both Facebook pages (through Metricool), and the practice's own new-patient and attrition data into one monthly dashboard, plus a weekly update to the owners: completed, in progress, waiting on, coming next, and items to watch.

Why it connects: for the first time, marketing activity could be traced to calls, online bookings and new patients. That's how I could see that online bookings (143 in September) now far outnumber calls from the website (46), which changes how the phone numbers should be read.

6. Separate strategies for two different offices

Lebanon is established, so its content focused on retention, treatment follow-through and new families. Franklin is growing, so its content focused on direct booking, reactivation and introducing the team. I also secured a Lebanon Christmas Parade sponsorship and produced flyers for a Franklin Chamber of Commerce partnership.

What would have drawn in more patients

With more time and support, these are the moves that would have produced the biggest gains:

  • Add the new domain to Search Console right away. Until it's added, Google's reports on the new address are incomplete, which slows every SEO decision.
  • Ask every happy patient for a review. A simple text after each visit is the single cheapest way to climb the map results. Reviews are the strongest local signal a practice controls.
  • Turn ads back on, narrowly. January and February, when paid search was running, were the practice's strongest months for bookings. A small budget aimed only at "emergency dentist" and Franklin new-patient searches would likely pay for itself.
  • A new-patient welcome series by email and text. It reduces no-shows and keeps new patients coming back for their second visit, which is where retention is won or lost.
  • Real photos of the real team. Patients choose people. Doctor headshots, bios and team photos make the Google profiles and website feel like a practice, not a template.
  • Connect the front desk to marketing. Asking "how did you hear about us?" at every booking, and sharing the answers, would show exactly which channels bring patients in.
  • Commit to the plan long enough to work. SEO and content compound over six to twelve months. Judging them at four months is like judging a garden in its first week.
