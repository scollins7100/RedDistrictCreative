Facebook Is Not Your Marketing Plan

By Stephanie Collins · October 14, 2026 · 5 minute read

Ask a small business owner how their marketing is going, and there's a good chance the answer is about Facebook. "We post a few times a week." "We boosted a post last month." "Our posts don't get many likes anymore."

Posting isn't a plan. It's an activity. And for most local businesses, it's the activity furthest from the moment a customer actually decides to buy.

Where customers look when they're ready to buy

Think about the last time you needed a dentist, a landscaper or a place to buy a tree. You probably didn't scroll Facebook hoping one would appear. You searched Google. You looked at the map, compared star ratings, clicked a website, and called or booked.

That's where buying decisions happen: search results, Google Maps, reviews and your website. Facebook is where people scroll when they're relaxing. It can build familiarity, but it rarely closes the deal on its own.

The order that works

I build a business's digital presence in this order, because each layer depends on the one before it:

  1. Your website. The one place you own. It has to load fast, answer questions and make booking or buying easy. Every other channel should lead here.
  2. Google. Your Business Profile, search results, Maps, reviews and, more and more, AI answers. Google decides whether to show you based largely on your website and profile.
  3. Email. Customers who already know you, reached for free, with no algorithm deciding who sees it.
  4. Social media. Familiarity, trust and community, once the foundation is in place.

Most small businesses do this backwards. They pour time into social first, while their Google profile is half finished and their website still lists last year's hours.

What social media is actually good for

None of this means social media doesn't matter. Done well, it can be a cornerstone. It keeps you familiar between purchases, shows the people behind the business, and gives customers a reason to choose you over a stranger.

I've seen it firsthand. At a dental practice I worked with, posts introducing real team members consistently outperformed promotional posts. People respond to people. A stock photo with "Book now!" on it doesn't build trust. A real hygienist patients already know and like does.

Three questions before you post

Social media works when every post has a job. Before you publish anything, ask:

  1. What is this channel for? Awareness, trust, event turnout or bookings. Pick one goal per channel.
  2. Who is it for? New customers, regulars, or the community. Each needs a different message.
  3. What should they do next? Visit the website, book, come to an event, or leave a review. A post with no next step is a dead end.

If you can't answer all three, you're not doing social media marketing. You're just posting.

It runs on real content

The other thing social media needs is a steady supply of real photos and video: your products, your space, your team and your events. That content has to come from inside the business, because that's where the story is. A good marketer turns it into a plan, writes the posts, schedules them and measures what they do. But no strategy can invent a behind-the-scenes moment that never got captured.

Build the foundation first

If your website is slow, your Google profile is incomplete and you've never emailed your customers, more Facebook posts won't fix your marketing. Start with the foundation. Get found where people are ready to buy. Then let social media do what it does best: remind people why they already like you.
